## 原文

Daily French Practice
This is an essential part of my practice that is done every day. Doing it daily is not only essential for your exposure but also necessary for some of the learning resources that are involved. Daily practice consists of:

- Vocabulary with Anki
- Verb Conjugation
- Number Practice
- Listening Practice
- Reading / Writing Practice
- Simple Writing Practice

### Vocabulary with Anki

This is just about learning words in French. For this I use an Anki Vocabulary Deck.
Anki is a flashcard app that runs on your laptop. Decks can be created on Anki which contain not just the flashcards, but how they look and when they are shown.
The specific vocabulary deck I have is a customized one here: Shared French Resources > Anki Vocabulary Deck.apkg
This deck includes words I've learnt from the last 10 months, lots of which came from TCF past exams, it's well over 3000 words so you will take a while to go through it!

To use it, install Anki and then import the Anki Vocabulary Deck into the program. The deck itself teaches you words using spaced repetition.
For a given day, you will learn some new words, but also review words you have learnt previously.
The application also includes buttons shown with the flashcards that indicate how easy/hard it was to remember the word, the option you select affects how many days will pass before the application shows that word to you again.

Because of this, you should do vocabulary sitting down in front of your computer, it is not designed to be done on the go (you won't retain it as well).

It's essential that you do vocabulary daily. The spaced repetition only works if you are using it on a daily basis. Additionally, the words to review are cumulative, that is, if you don't do the vocabulary today, the words you have to review will just be added to the next day and so on.
If you're not careful you can end up having 200 words to review in a single sitting!
I may skimp on other things on this list, but this cumulative aspect makes it so that I NEVER skip vocabulary.

#### When doing a vocabulary session

When you learn a word, learn the grammar around the word
- If it's a noun, the article: the gender, the partitive countable
- If it's an adjective: the different forms - gender and number agreement
- If it's a verb: learn the prepositions it uses (See Word Differences Chat in ChatGPT/AI prompts), its conjugation (covered in daily practice)

If you learn a word that has a similar meaning to another word you've learnt before, learn and understand the nuance between the two of them e.g. facilement vs aisément. (See Word Differences Chat in ChatGPT/AI prompts)

Take note of the new words you learn each day, and words you struggled to remember or spell (for example on a separate document like a list), this list of words will be used as part of Daily Writing / Reading practice.

#### What do I do when I've gone through the vocabulary deck?

There are 4000 words in there, so it will take you a while to do so, but for this case you should check out: Tracking New Words.

### Verb Conjugation

There are several various ways to conjugate a verb in French, but they aren't all necessarily distinct. A good way to learn conjugations is to practice a few of them daily.
Note: I recommend learning the different tenses of verbs first before starting any conjugation i.e. when to use passé composé vs imparfait etc., see Pre-TCF Evening Practice > Learning Verb Tenses

The Excel sheet Shared French Resources > Conjugation Learning Tracking.xlsx contains all the different conjugations that exist for verbs, organized with an example word. You can use this Excel spreadsheet to track your learning:
- Pick 3 to do for the day
- Use https://www.scholingua.com/en/fr/conjugation-trainer to do the practice
- Update the spreadsheet with your last practice and confidence level

I generally did two sets of SchoLingua practice:
- First set would be 3 verbs from the spreadsheet using all the simple (i.e. one word) French tenses
- The next pass would be for compound tenses, for which I would only use a single verb

Doing this practice every day will make verb conjugation intuitive and easy!

### Number Practice

This trains your ability to hear and understand French numbers on the fly, something that is very difficult without practice. The process is simple:
- Go to https://langpractice.com/french/numbers/listening#1,0,10000
- Practice for 5 - 10 mins every day, you can do it on the go, while chilling etc

### Listening Practice

You can only really start listening practice at level A2, or when you have learnt close to 1000 words, starting any time before that will be extremely difficult.

For listening practice, you can use LingQ - https://www.lingq.com/en/
Do their daily practice lesson, this will keep you occupied for 60 days.

After that, I did mostly passive listening practice i.e. make sure that the majority of things you watch and listen to are in French
- Watch your favourite series or movies in French dubs, with French / English subtitles
- Try to find French Youtube channels that match your interest
- Find French music you like
- Use the InnerFrench (https://innerfrench.com/) podcast for practice at an intermediate level

I rarely ever did active listening practice (outside of doing TCF past exams), but I am someone who watches a LOT of videos so I can easily get 1-3 hours of practice every day.

### Speaking Practice

I did this by speaking to ChatGPT in Voice Mode, talk about anything and everything
- What I'm currently doing, e.g. while cooking dinner
- How my day went etc

The benefit is that you can control the speed it speaks to you and you can ask it immediately how to say a given French sentence or word.
This will be very hard to do in the beginning, start just about the same time as you are doing your Listening practice.

### Reading / Writing Practice

I did this as part of my Evening practice during my learning phase (Month 1-3). This will take quite a few hours in the beginning (as there is so much to learn). Talked about in more detail in the Pre-TCF Evening Practice.

### Simple Writing Practice

This is the vocabulary practice I did while I was actively preparing for the TCF.
I couldn't do the above Reading/Writing practice because it took way too much time during the day (time I could better spend studying the actual TCF exam).
This just allows me to spell and use my daily vocabulary words.
Spelling is incredibly important, you do not want to make spelling errors, and you also get a glimpse into conjugation for new verbs.

To do this practice:
- Collect the list of words you tracked during your vocabulary practice
- Send them into your Daily Spelling Practice Chat (see ChatGPT AI prompts to configure the chat)
- Copy the output table to an Excel spreadsheet
- White out (i.e. set font color to white) the word and the French sentence columns
- In a separate document, write out your French sentences based on the English translation
- Unwhite the French sentences and words to ensure that you used the correct word and that your sentence is grammatically correct
- If you don't understand a sentence, deep dive and understand why
